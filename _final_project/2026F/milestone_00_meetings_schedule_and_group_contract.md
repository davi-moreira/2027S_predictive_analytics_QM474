## What this milestone is for

M00 does two jobs at once, and both are about making your group work before the technical work starts.

The first is **scheduling your Round 01 meetings** with the TA and with Professor Moreira. These meetings are where your prediction problem gets pressure-tested early, while changing direction is still cheap.

The second is your **Group Contract**. Predictive analytics projects fail more often because of broken team communication than because of broken code. Writing down how your group will communicate, divide work, make decisions, and handle a teammate who goes quiet takes twenty minutes now and saves the project later. It is also the document Professor Moreira will refer back to if a group escalates a contribution dispute, so the version you submit is the one that counts.

## Part 1 — Group Contract

Download the **Group Contract template** from the Milestone 00 assignment on Brightspace, complete every section as a group, have **all members sign**, and export it as a PDF.

The template asks your group to agree on:

1. **Members and contact information** — name, Purdue email, and preferred contact method for every member.
2. **Communication norms** — your primary channel and the response time everyone commits to.
3. **Meeting cadence** — when your group meets, and how meetings get scheduled.
4. **Roles** — who takes point on data, modeling, writing, poster design, and submission. Roles can rotate; write down how.
5. **Decision-making** — how your group settles a disagreement about direction.
6. **Work distribution and deadlines** — how tasks get assigned and how you track them.
7. **Accountability** — what your group does when a member misses an internal deadline, and at what point Professor Moreira is brought in.
8. **Signatures** — every member signs and dates.

Fill it in honestly rather than generically. A contract that says "we will communicate well" helps nobody; one that says "Slack, replies within 24 hours on weekdays, and we escalate to Professor Moreira if a member is unreachable for five days" is a document your group can actually use.

## Part 2 — Round 01 Meeting Requests

Your group schedules **two** Round 01 meetings:

Export each sent email as a PDF and upload both.

## Tips and Common Pitfalls

- **Send the scheduling emails early.** You are proposing times to two people with full calendars. A request sent on the deadline gets you a meeting outside your window.
- **Offer real options.** Three or four concrete date-and-time slots per email, not "let us know when you are free."
- **Copy all group members on both emails.** This is a stated requirement, and it also means nobody misses the confirmed time.
- **Put your section number in the email.** There are two sections; without it the reply is slower.
- **Do not skip the accountability section of the contract.** It is the section groups most want to leave vague and the one that matters most if something goes wrong.
- **Use Purdue email**, not personal accounts. External addresses are more likely to be filtered.
- **Decide your designated uploader now** and write it into the contract, so there is no confusion at any future submission.
