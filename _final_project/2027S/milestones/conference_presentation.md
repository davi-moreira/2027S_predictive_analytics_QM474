## What this milestone is for

This is the day the project goes public. **Presenting at the Spring Undergraduate Research Conference is required for everyone in the course.** You stand at your poster, explain your work to faculty, classmates, judges and visitors, and answer their questions, using the plan you wrote in Milestone 11.

## What to do

1. **Plan around the date.** The poster session may not fall at our usual class time. Tell your other instructors in advance about any conflict, and let Professor Moreira know right away if a problem comes up.
2. **Present.** Be at your poster for your session, with your Milestone 11 pitches ready.
3. **Take your photo.** Take a photo of yourself in front of your group's poster, with the poster visible behind you.
4. **Upload it** as a PDF.

There is no class on Wednesday, April 14, the day after the conference: use it to rest and to catch up on your other courses.

## What to submit

Each of you uploads a photo of yourself in front of your group's poster, saved as `NN_presentation_photo.pdf` (for example `03_presentation_photo.pdf`). The photo is your proof that you presented.

## How it is graded

The conference presentation is worth 20% of your Final Project grade. Separately, after the conference, Professor Moreira and the TA evaluate your final poster with the poster rubric (the Instructor/TA Evaluation, another 20% of the project).

## Tips

- Take the photo early in your session, not when the room is emptying.
- Make sure the poster in the photo is legible enough to identify as yours.
- Check the official conference program ahead of time for your session time and location.

Conference information: <https://www.purdue.edu/undergrad-research/conferences/index.php>

*The Final Project Overview PDF has every date, the meeting rounds, and how the project is graded.*
