## What this milestone is for

Each of you rates, confidentially, how every teammate contributed. It keeps everyone accountable, it rewards the people who carried their share, and it lets Professor Moreira see how the work was really divided.

Peer *evaluation* is not peer *review*. Peer review (due Sunday, April 11) was your group reviewing other groups' posters. Here you rate **your own teammates**.

## What to do

Open the form on Brightspace and evaluate **every other member of your group**. There is no self-evaluation. For each teammate, give five ratings from 1 to 5 and two short comments.

| Dimension | What it measures |
|---|---|
| Commitment | Showed up, engaged with the work, and stayed involved through the whole project, not only at deadlines. |
| Technical contribution | Contributed real substance to the analysis: data work, modeling, evaluation, or the technical writing that depends on them. |
| Communication | Replied within the group's agreed time, kept others informed, and raised problems early rather than silently. |
| Dependability | Delivered what they committed to, by when they committed to it. |
| Fair share of the work | Took a share proportionate to the group, neither coasting nor quietly absorbing everyone else's tasks. |

The scale is **1** far below expectations, **2** below, **3** met expectations, **4** above, **5** far above. A 3 is the normal, healthy rating for a teammate who did their share. Keep 1 and 5 for cases you can point to a specific reason for.

Then, for each teammate, write **one specific strength** (something concrete they did well) and **one specific area for improvement** (something concrete they could do differently). "Great teammate" and "did nothing" are both unusable. Specific comments protect both you and the person you rate.

## What to submit

Your completed form, individually, by the deadline. It covers your Final Project group and your Course Case Competition team; the Poster-to-Product share is assessed with the same questions for your sprint team.

## How it is graded

Your peer-evaluation score is computed **for you**, from the average of the ratings your teammates give you, with light moderation by Professor Moreira where ratings look strategically inflated or deflated. Two members of the same group can therefore receive different scores. If you do not submit, **your own** peer-evaluation score goes down, not your teammates'.

Your ratings are confidential: only Professor Moreira and the TA see individual evaluations. Aggregated feedback may be returned to the team.

## Tips

- Rate the contribution, not the friendship.
- Use your group contract (Milestone 00) as the yardstick: it says what your group agreed to.
- Write the comments first, then the numbers. The numbers come out fairer.
