## What this milestone is for

Halfway through the project, each of you rates, confidentially, how every teammate is contributing so far. The point is to **catch problems while they can still be fixed**. By now your group has written its contract, held its first round of meetings and built its first model, so you know how the work is really being shared, and there are still five weeks before the final poster is due.

This midpoint evaluation does not affect anyone's grade beyond completion. The final peer evaluation (Friday, April 30) is the graded one, and it asks the same questions, so this is also a preview of how you will be rated.

## What to do

Open the form on Brightspace and evaluate **every other member of your group** on the same five dimensions as the final evaluation — commitment, technical contribution, communication, dependability, and fair share of the work — each from 1 (far below expectations) to 5 (far above), with 3 as the normal rating for a teammate who does their share.

For each teammate, write **one specific strength** and **one specific thing they could do differently from now on**. Then answer two questions about the group as a whole:

1. Is anything getting in the way of your group's work right now (a missing member, an unclear split of tasks, a conflict, a technical block)?
2. Would you like Professor Moreira to check in with your group? You can say yes confidentially.

## What happens next

Professor Moreira reads every form. A group gets a check-in meeting when any member asks for one, when a member is rated 2 or lower on average by the others, or when the comments point to a problem. In that meeting the group revisits its contract (Milestone 00) and agrees on what changes. Ratings stay confidential: no one sees who rated them what. Comments may be shared with the team only in summarized, anonymous form.

## How it is graded

Completion: one submitted form, with every teammate rated and commented, by the deadline. The ratings you receive do not count toward your grade at this point.

## Tips

- Be honest now. A problem raised at the midpoint can be fixed; the same problem raised only at the end can only be graded.
- Write the comments first, then the numbers.
- Use your group contract as the yardstick: it says what your group agreed to.
