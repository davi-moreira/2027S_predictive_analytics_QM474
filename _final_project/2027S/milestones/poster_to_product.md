## What this milestone is for

After the conference, your team works like a product team for two weeks. You turn your validated model into something a business decision-maker can use: **an interactive product you build with AI** (a dashboard, a decision calculator or what-if tool, a small web app or an interactive report) **at a link anyone can open**, plus **a one-page brief** and **a three-minute video**. You test it with real users, present it at a Demo Day, and publish it under your own name. You leave with a product you can show an employer, real feedback, and practice in checking what an AI builds against your own evidence.

You build with two AI tools that Purdue gives you at no cost: **Google Antigravity**, an AI build tool that writes, runs and tests your product on your laptop, and **Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat**, an AI chat assistant for thinking, writing and checking. You sign in to both with your **Purdue account**. The AI Access Guide in the Poster-to-Product module shows how, in about 20 minutes. The build tool is approved by Purdue for public data only; if your project's data are not public, tell Professor Moreira before the kickoff. ## How you will work: the Launch Sprint

The sprint follows the design sprint that product teams use: **Map, Sketch, Decide, Prototype, Test**, then **Launch**, a **Demo Day** and a **Retro**. Each teammate takes a role (Product Owner, Data Steward, Build Lead or Launch Lead) and signs off one part of the product before launch. Every session starts with a five-minute stand-up, and your team keeps a Sprint Log of its decisions, the prompts that mattered and every check you ran on the AI's work.

## What to do, session by session

1. **Wednesday, April 14 to Friday, April 16, on your own: Onboard.** Sign in to both AI tools, make a first small build with the practice data, and agree on roles. Your Product Owner creates the team folder, "Team NN – Poster to Product", in Purdue OneDrive and shares it with the team. **I1 Get Your Keys** is due Sunday, April 18.
2. **Friday, April 16 (Session 1, kickoff): Map.** Choose one decision-maker and one decision, three risks, and the three questions your product must answer in 60 seconds. By Sunday, April 18, your Product Owner emails your section's TA to book your Round 03 Sprint Review.
3. **Monday, April 19 (Session 2): Sketch and Decide.** Sketch eight ideas each, vote, and turn the winner into a storyboard. The Data Steward runs the export cell (on Brightspace) at the end of your final notebook and checks that the cross-validated mean it prints matches your poster. **G1 Sprint Blueprint** and **I2** are due Tuesday, April 20.
4. **Wednesday, April 21 (Session 3): Prototype.** Bring a laptop. The Build Lead drives the AI build tool, everyone reviews, and the team builds version one, using only your own numbers, at a link anyone can open. **I3** is due that night.
5. **Thursday, April 22 or Friday, April 23: Round 03 Sprint Review with your TA (20 minutes, on Teams).** Like every meeting round this term, it opens with a presentation your team prepares, here 10 minutes, and every member presents a part: your decision and what the conference taught you, your evidence, a live demo of the prototype, what you checked and learned, and your team. Your slides, the prototype link and your AI check log are due at 11:59 PM the day before the meeting. You leave with three action items.
6. **Friday, April 23 (Session 4): Test.** First half, the truth test: three numbers checked against the notebook, and the link opened signed out on another device. Second half, the user test: two classmates from another team play your decision-maker for 60 seconds each. **G3 Test Report** and **I4** are due Sunday, April 25.
7. **Monday, April 26 (Session 5): Launch Day.** Finish the brief and the video, run the final checks, sign off by role, and submit the **G4 Launch Package** by 11:59 PM. After that, nothing at your link changes.
8. **Wednesday, April 28 (Session 6) and Friday, April 30 (Session 7): Demo Days.** Half the teams in each section present on each day, in a random order posted on Brightspace: a five-minute presentation built around a live demo that makes the case for your product's value to the decision-maker (**G5**). Everyone reviews every other team in the section in about 30 seconds (**I6**). Your own live link, **I5 Go Live**, is due Wednesday, April 28.
9. **Friday, April 30, the last class: close.** After the last team, you start the Reflection Survey and then the final peer evaluation in class; both are due at 11:59 PM.

## How it is graded

Poster-to-Product is 5% of the course grade, scored out of 100 points:

- **30, individual milestones:** I1 to I6, 5 points each, graded by the TAs.
- **50, team milestones:** G1 Sprint Blueprint 5, G2 Sprint Review 10, G3 Test Report 5, G4 Launch Package 15 and G5 Demo Day 15, graded by Professor Moreira and the TAs. The Launch Package is scored with the four criteria of the judging rubric (decision usefulness, clarity and ease of use, evidence and honesty, communication) plus the launch requirements. On Demo Day, Professor Moreira scores your presentation (10 points) and your classmates' reviews count for 5.
- **20, peer evaluation** of your sprint team, in the final peer evaluation.

**The Launch Award.** Every member of the top three teams in each section earns 5 percentage points of extra credit on the final course grade. ## Rules and tips

- Use only your project's real data and results, and check that your data may be shared before you give them to the AI and before you publish.
- Never give any AI tool personal information or anything about your classmates.
- Do not open or re-test your test set: the product shows what your notebook already validated.
- Describe top features as what the model relies on, never as causes.
- Save the prompts and checks that matter in your Sprint Log: the chat assistant deletes its history after 30 days.
- Stuck? Ask your TA.
